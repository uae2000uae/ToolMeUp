How to use

  1. Enter your current tire size and the size you're considering, exactly as printed on the sidewall — e.g. 225/45R17, LT265/70R17 or 33x12.50R20.
  2. Compare sizes shows every dimension, a to-scale drawing and a verdict. Most makers recommend keeping the overall diameter within ±3%.
  3. Speedometer shows your real speed for any reading, assuming your speedometer was accurate on the current size.
How do I read a tire size like 225/45R17?

225 is the tread width in millimetres, 45 is the sidewall height as a percentage of that width (45% of 225 mm ≈ 101 mm), R means radial construction, and 17 is the wheel diameter in inches.

Why does a bigger tire make my speedometer read low?

A taller tire travels further per turn. The speedometer counts turns, so at a reading of 100 the car is actually going faster. Smaller tires do the opposite.
